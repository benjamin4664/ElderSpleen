MANIFESTO

ElderSpleen rests on one conviction: the reach of great texts extends beyond mere academic preservation. Far from being simple objects of scholarship, these words retain an undiminished sharpness and stand as conceptual landmarks for illuminating the present.

At the heart of the project lies a fertile tension:

  • The Spleen — that contemporary melancholy, the vertigo of a saturated world, the white noise of our era.
  • The Elders — the figure of the Ancients, those voices that have already passed through chaos and come to illuminate our own shadows.

This spleen is neither a taste for darkness nor a resignation: it is the starting point of a cathartic effort. The aim is to move beyond vertigo through thought and sound. When reason and poetry take hold of our fears, the tension unravels. What once suffocated becomes a space of freedom.

ElderSpleen thus asserts itself as a genuine sonic and conceptual experience. The music refuses gratuitous ornamentation: it sculpts a relief in which the text takes shape. 

A note on language.  If the texts are written and spoken in (old) French. No translation is provided — and none is needed. The meaning of these works is to travels through tone, through rhythm, through the weight a voice gives to silence, through the tension music builds and releases. A listener who doesn't understand French is not excluded from this experience — they are invited into a different way of receiving it: one closer to how meaning first reaches us before language, through cadence and feeling. In that sense, ElderSpleen asks to be felt as much as understood.
